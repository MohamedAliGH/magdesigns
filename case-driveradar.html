DriveRadar · SEW-Eurodrive · B2B SaaS / IIoT

A factory’s failing machinery, legible at a glance

All the data, almost none of the legibility. A map and table where every asset looked equal; failing units didn’t stand out. A missed warning isn’t bad UX — it’s a stopped production line.

ShippedProduct / UI designer — owned the asset-overview, asset-detail, and measurement views.

Role
Product / UI Designer

The problem

(04.1) — Problem

The platform was built around the data the sensors produce, not the decision the operator has to make. Raw vibration plots — accurate, but unreadable at a glance; every measurement at the same visual weight; no hierarchy of concern.

Design around the decision, not the data — demote the spectrum so a failing gearbox surfaces.

Evidence & discovery

(04.2) — Evidence

For a metric-light craft case the visuals are the proof. The redesign reorganises three levels of the product, each answering exactly one question and handing off cleanly to the next.

  1. 01Fleet"Where do I look?" — which assets have alerts, ranked by severity.
  2. 02Asset"What's wrong with this machine?" — health → measurements → components → service.
  3. 03Signal"Is this getting worse?" — one measurement trended against its threshold.

The approach

(04.3) — Approach

Asset detail reorganised around the operator’s mental model of the machine: availability up top, then key measurements as scannable states (“everything is fine” vs. a flagged value), then components with vibration status, then service info — a 3D model anchoring the operator in the physical machine.

The design

(04.4) — Solution

From “can you read this spectrum?” to “is it trending toward the red line?” Raw vibration plots became a clean time-series with the threshold line and week/month/year ranges. The specialist’s spectral detail still exists — it moved one click deeper.

Outcome & what I'd measure

(04.5) — Outcome

The honest framing for a craft case: rather than invent a percentage, state the designed improvement plainly — fleet triage moved from scanning an undifferentiated table to a single severity-sorted view. The number worth instrumenting next is time-to-locate a flagged asset, old versus new.

Trade-off & reflection

(04.6) — Reflection

The decisions made under constraint: severity over completeness (a specialist’s raw reading is one click deeper — the right call, since triage is many users every day and deep analysis is few users occasionally), and readable trend over raw fidelity. What I’d sharpen: validate alert thresholds and colour categories with the engineers who live in alarm fatigue.
