easyCredit · Fintech · Web app

Removing friction from a high-stakes credit funnel

A carousel was hiding the journey users had already committed to. The fix was not a restyle — it was making the full path honest and visible, without adding or removing a single required step.

ShippedSenior Product Designer — owned the audit, exploration, n=6 usability test, and shipped design.

Sr. Product Designer

The problem

(01.1) — Problem

Steps lived behind a horizontal swipe; discoverability relied on a gesture many users never made. People committed, then discovered more steps late — and that surprise broke momentum at the worst point in a high-anxiety flow.

Not a styling problem — a trust problem. Hidden effort costs belief, and belief is what carries a user through a high-stakes flow.

Evidence & discovery

(01.2) — Evidence

Three inputs, not a pattern preference: a funnel audit located the drop-off precisely at the carousel step; a small moderated test explained why; and a WCAG review confirmed carousels score poorly on discoverability, keyboard, and screen-reader support.

The approach

(01.3) — Approach

I explored three directions and killed two — an enhanced carousel (more controls, same comprehension problem), a full-screen stepper (hid the full journey, the exact problem, and needed custom components), and vertical disclosure cards (all steps visible, details on demand). The cards shipped.

The design

(01.4) — Solution

Every step visible at a glance, details on demand. Vertical disclosure cards: 100% of steps shown immediately, each expanding for detail only when needed — scroll-based, keyboard- and screen-reader-accessible, no swipe to discover.

Outcome & what I'd measure

(01.5) — Outcome

Shipped to production on an existing design-system component — faster to ship, lower to maintain, accessible by inheritance. The pre-redesign drop-off concentrated at the carousel step; the post-launch result is being instrumented, so this case states the measurement plan rather than a lift it cannot yet defend honestly.

How I’d prove it — projected / hypotheses

  • Stage-completion rateprimary — variant vs. carousel, holdout split
  • Time-to-first-doubtsecondary — momentum signal
  • Accessibility conformancekeyboard + screen-reader pass

Trade-off & reflection

(01.6) — Reflection

The trade-off I chose: visibility over visual novelty. A carousel looks more modern; honest visibility serves the brand better, because "easy" is a core value and a transparent funnel makes it literal. What I would measure next: stage-completion rate of this variant against the carousel in a holdout split, with time-to-first-doubt as a secondary signal.
